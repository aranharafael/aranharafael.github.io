> Course on introductory physics (mechanics) for first-year undergraduate students.

## Goals

Newtonian mechanics is presented as a theory built upon a small number of principles (definitions and axioms), from which all other course topics—forces, equilibrium and dynamics, energy, collisions, rigid bodies, and gravitation—are derived, rather than treated as independent subjects. By the end of the course, students should be able to state and demonstrate all the axioms, definitions, lemmas, and corollaries of mechanics, as well as solve problems by systematically applying its laws. Furthermore, students will become proficient in manipulating vectors and using differential and integral calculus, recognizing these as an inherent part of the language of physics. In this way, students will begin their journey toward understanding the fundamental concepts of basic physics, laying the groundwork for further study in more advanced areas, such as thermodynamics, waves, electromagnetism, and modern physics.
