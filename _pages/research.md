My research aims to make the mathematical tools used in the study of General Relativity not only more rigorous but at the same time reliable. The four areas below give different angles on a single line of work that brings together theoretical physics, mathematical methods and scientific computation.

> **Foundations of Numerical Relativity**

Numerical relativity solves Einstein’s equations on a computer to describe systems in which gravity is strong, such as black holes and neutron stars. Rather than focusing on the simulation of astrophysical events, my research investigates the mathematical foundations of the formalisms used to generate those simulations: the well-posedness of the formulations, the role of the constraint equations and of gauge choices, and how numerical errors arise and propagate throughout the evolution of several systems. The goal is to understand and quantify the quality of numerical results, providing criteria and diagnostics that make simulations more robust.
{: .notice}


> **Mathematical Methods of Gravitation**

General Relativity is formulated in the language of differential geometry, and the way its objects are represented shapes both the understanding of the theory and its practical calculations. This research analyzes mathematical structures and representations in General Relativity, such as tensors, tetrads and spinors, seeking formulations that are clear, consistent and useful for computation. It also investigates exact and approximate solutions of Einstein’s equations describing black holes, including rotating, charged and boosted configurations, as well as dynamical spacetimes that model gravitational radiation which are very important in the recent description of astrophysical scenarios. Finally, it also explores structural mathematical similarities (and differences) between the ones found in General Relativity and in another physical theories, such as Electromagnetism and Quantum mechanics.
{: .notice}


> **Numerical Analysis and Spectral Methods**

Spectral methods approximate solutions of differential equations by expansions in terms of specific types of orthogonal sets of functions, offering high accuracy when properly employed. This research studies the properties of these approximations in the light of functional analysis and Hilbert space theory: the choice and conditioning of bases, the stability of computations, and also the derivation of analytical expressions that replace costly numerical procedures. These are tools of general interest, with natural applications to problems not only in Gravitation but in other areas of research such as Hydrodynamics.
{: .notice}

> **Teaching Theoretical Physics with Mathematical Rigor**

My teaching activity always followed the same principles of my research: theoretical physics should be presented with clarity and mathematical care, so that students understand not only how its methods work, but why they work. In this spirit, I am writing two series of textbooks on i) general methods of theoretical physics at both advanced undergraduate and introductory graduate levels. My main interests in this educational research cover Hilbert spaces, tensor analysis, classical mechanics and general relativity, organized mainly in the structure of definitions, propositions and theorems, making the logical architecture of each subject explicit; ii) introductory courses, where my main approach prioritizes the development of the ideas from fundamental principles as, for instance, in teaching mechanics from Newton’s laws before kinematics, so that students see physics as a coherent deductive theory from the very beginning. Finally, I am committed to training students from the early years of their undergraduate studies through research projects, introducing them gradually to these mathematical structures and physical intuition and consequently preparing them for research in any kind of the vast areas of theoretical physics.
{: .notice}
