My research aims to make the mathematical and numerical tools used in the study of gravitation more rigorous and reliable. The three areas below are different angles on a single line of work that brings together theoretical physics, mathematics and computation.

> **Foundations of Numerical Relativity**

Numerical relativity solves Einstein’s equations on a computer to describe systems in which gravity is strong, such as black holes and neutron stars. Rather than focusing on the simulation of astrophysical events, this line investigates the mathematical foundations of those simulations: the well-posedness of the formulations, the role of the constraint equations and of gauge choices, and how numerical errors arise and propagate throughout an evolution. The goal is to understand and quantify the quality of numerical results, providing criteria and diagnostics that make simulations more reliable.
{: .notice}


> **Mathematical Methods of Gravitation**

General Relativity is formulated in the language of differential geometry, and the way its objects are represented shapes both the understanding of the theory and its practical calculations. This line studies mathematical structures and representations in gravitation, such as tensors, tetrads and spinors, seeking formulations that are clear, consistent and useful for computation. It also investigates exact and approximate solutions of Einstein’s equations describing black holes, including rotating, charged and boosted configurations, as well as dynamical spacetimes that model gravitational radiation and the recoil of black holes formed in astrophysical processes. Finally, it explores structural analogies between gravitation and other physical theories, such as electromagnetism and quantum mechanics, which help illuminate aspects of each.
{: .notice}


> **Numerical Analysis and Spectral Methods**

Spectral methods approximate solutions of differential equations by expansions in bases of functions, offering high accuracy when properly employed. This line studies the properties of these approximations in the light of functional analysis and Hilbert space theory: the choice and conditioning of bases, the stability of computations, and the derivation of analytical expressions that replace costly numerical procedures. These are tools of general interest, with natural applications to problems in gravitation..
{: .notice}

> **Teaching Theoretical Physics with Mathematical Rigor**

My teaching activity follows the same principle as my research: theoretical physics should be presented with clarity and mathematical care, so that students understand not only how its methods work, but why they work. In this spirit, I am writing a series of textbooks on the methods of theoretical physics at an intermediate level, a range that is often missing between introductory texts and specialized monographs. My courses and lecture notes cover Hilbert spaces, tensor analysis, classical mechanics at both introductory and advanced levels, and general relativity, organized in the structure of definitions, propositions and theorems, making the logical architecture of each subject explicit. In introductory courses, I favor approaches that start from the fundamental principles, as in teaching mechanics from Newton’s laws before kinematics, so that students see physics as a coherent deductive theory from the very beginning. Finally, I am committed to training students from the early years of their undergraduate studies through research projects, introducing them gradually to these mathematical and physical foundations and preparing them for research in gravitation.
{: .notice}
